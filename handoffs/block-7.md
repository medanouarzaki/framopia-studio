## Decisions made (and why)

1. **Subtitle cards hold one word** (user ruling, session 6). A two-word card renders both words the moment the first is spoken, so the second is on screen before it is said — every time, pooled median 0.410 s of anticipation. No retiming fixes it; the fault is grouping. This amends PROJECT_SPEC §5's "groups of 1–2 words". Cards went 190 → 343.

2. **A card too short for the entrance gets a faster entrance, never a dropped word** (conversation ruling, session 9). One-word grouping put 120 of 343 cards under `introS + minHoldS`, and an unbuilt card is a spoken word with nothing on screen. The instance's intro is time-stretched to fit, floored at **2 frames (0.0667 s)** — chosen, not measured. 120 shortened, 28 on the floor, 0 words left without a card. **No keyframe is edited**: this is layer time stretching, per TEMPLATE_LIBRARY_GUIDE §5.

3. **A card holds until the next word begins**, bounded by `MAX_SUBTITLE_HOLD_S` = 1.20 s (chosen, not measured). It removed **17.25 s of blank screen** on `vitasilk`, down to 0.66 s. It did **not** reduce the short-card count at all — the old rule already extended to the next card whenever the floor was unreachable, so short cards stay exactly as short. Arithmetic, not disappointment.

4. **Text is measured inside After Effects, by After Effects** (conversation ruling, session 5). Session 4 stopped correctly: the glyph-outline machinery Block 6 was believed to have built does not exist — it produced a measurement and discarded the script. `FONT_METRICS` holds vertical ink extents only, no advance widths. Width is harder than height: it needs per-glyph advances, kerning and Arabic positional shaping, where a naive implementation mis-wraps Arabic silently. Any library models what AE will draw; AE reports what AE draws, and the builder is already inside AE when it needs the answer. `sourceRectAtTime` at an explicit time. 193 cards measured in 2.9 s — fast enough that a production build need not design around it. **No new dependency.**

5. **A card too wide wraps to a second line at the same size**, `SUBTITLE_SAFE_WIDTH` = 1940 px (chosen, not measured). Under two-word grouping: 140 one-line, 53 wrapped, 0 single-word overflows at card level. Under one-word grouping wrapping nearly vanished — 9 wrapped cards down to 1. **Seven single long words still exceed the bound after breaking** (`polynucléotides`, `mésothérapie` ×2, `hyaluronique` ×2, `matrddadich`); emitted whole and flagged. The user was offered the trade-offs — shrink, widen the margin, hyphenate — and it remains open.

6. **`prop.value` returns the value at the project's current time indicator, not at any time the caller intends** (session 3). `sub_pop`'s `TXT_MAIN` first read back at y 750, opacity 0 — the start of its intro, because the CTI sat on frame 0. Computing from it would have put **every subtitle 50 px low, silently**. Every audited property now carries both `value` and `valueAtSampleTime`; the settled baseline is y 700, and the placed card landed at 2480.39990234375 against a target of 2480.4.

7. **The baseline test was nearly wrong in the other direction** (session 5). Comparing a one-line rect top against a wrapped rect top flags 19 of 53 as moved, and `Status: PROBLEM — wrapping moved the baseline` was nearly reported on it. `top` is the distance to the top of the ink, so it moves whenever the break sends the tallest glyph to line two — `marque Vita` reads −261.4 then −190.3 purely because the capital V left line one. The honest test is the wrapped top against line one measured alone; nothing moved on any of the 53.

8. **Images are always placed in the top-left corner** (user ruling, session 9). This retires zone-based automatic image placement. His reason: in this format the corner is reliably empty, and the only real constraint is the speaker's face. **Verified beyond the reel it was ruled on** — the corner clears the face by 834–995 px on all five reels; zero concurrent slot pairs exist, so nothing stacks. It costs 3–7% of size on four slots versus the free-search best, which he was told. Jitter is one-sided so it can only shrink, never grow onto the face. The zone machinery is **retired, not removed** — manual zones and future formats still use it, as Block 6 did with torso derivation.

9. **Every image is framed** (user ruling, session 9). The card presentation on every slot, regardless of the cutout gate. **Consequence stated rather than acted on:** the gate, its four metrics and the BiRefNet sidecar now decide nothing about how an image renders, while still costing local CPU per reel. Nothing was deleted — PROJECT_SPEC §5 gives the editor an override and Block 8's panel is where presentation may become a per-slot choice again. `img_slide_left` is unused by automatic placement and stays in the library and the manifest.

10. **The image is scaled by its content, not its canvas** (session 7). The user's hypothesis was that images did not fill their frames. The files mostly do — originals reach a median 1.000 of the long edge. What is small is the **subject inside the picture**: median 0.701, as low as 0.548. Compounded by both image templates placing `IMG_MAIN` at 1000 px inside a 1200 comp. The worst slot was rendering its subject at **0.567 of the square it was given** — every effective image size published earlier in the block was overstated by roughly that factor. Position correction moves the **anchor point**, not the position: `img_slide_left` keyframes `IMG_MAIN`'s Position, and rewriting an animator's keyframes to shift a picture would be the wrong fix even where it worked.

11. **The card frame did not track its image, and the cause was parenting** (session 9). `IMG_MAIN` is parented to `CARD`, a fixed 1080 px layer. Content-aware scaling sized the content to 1000 px — right for a cutout, wrong for a card where the margin *is* picture — so the canvas rendered at 1000 × canvas/content and spilled whenever content filled under 0.926. Exactly 2 of 5 slots, matching the user's "some slots". Diagnosed by extending the audit to record parenting, and **fixed in the builder, not the template**.

12. **The zone rectangle was the binding constraint, not the hair rule** (session 8, correcting the conversation's own diagnosis). Measured relaxation value: the zone rectangle **1.78×** on its own, against 1.05–1.12× for every constant inside it, and `SCALE_JITTER` moving the ceiling not at all. The hair-versus-face distinction changes nothing until the zone is dropped, because the zone derives from the person mask. The conversation had proposed hair-versus-face as the fix; the measurement corrected it.

13. **The face-only mask is approved, on contact sheets** (user ruling, session 9/10). `HEAD_CATEGORIES` (1,3) → `FACE_CATEGORIES` (3,). On `vitasilk` the head mask reaches y 0.9521 and the face 0.5281 — 0.424 of frame height freed; subjects went from 265–534 px to 512–741 px. The user reviewed all five contact sheets at `benchmarks/results/latest-face/` and ruled no under-coverage. This is the same evidentiary standard Block 5's hair-is-head ruling was made on, and it partially reverses that ruling for image placement.

14. **The loosened variants nearly shipped an image across the speaker's face** (session 8). Built on the solved centre with a larger side, `loose` put `img001` and `face` put `img002` across it. **A square that fits somewhere is not a placement.** They now carry the position the ceiling actually found; verified after: 0 face overlaps, 0 frame escapes. This is precisely the failure the strict rule existed to prevent, and it is why the mask needed the user's eyes rather than the conversation's confidence.

15. **Nothing may ever be placed outside the frame** (session 8). Two escapes existed: `img001` crossed the left edge by 130 px and `img005` the top by 22 px, the second unseen by anyone. `satisfiesHardConstraints` checks `insideFrame` but only the solver called it; the variants reused the solved centre with a bigger side, bounded by nothing. Every path now ends in `fitInsideFrame`. Block 5 decision 10's property — jitter cannot escape by construction — still holds, because content-aware scaling changes scale inside the comp rather than the placed rect.

16. **The watermark: 216 px wide, deterministic corner, exactly 1.000 s, audio at −20 dB** (user rulings, sessions 1/2/10/11). Finder showed codec and container; everything load-bearing was measured. It is 30000/1001 — the same as the reels, no rate conversion — and Finder's `00:02` hides **61 frames**. The alpha is **premultiplied against black**: 2.96M partial-alpha pixels, 0.0000% violating the premultiplied prediction, and the artwork is essentially white (mean 252.7 over 16.8M opaque pixels), so a straight reading would leave edge pixels near 255 — they sit at 0.9854 of their alpha. On dark artwork the tool reports undecided instead. AE confirms `alphaMode` 5414 = `AlphaMode.PREMULTIPLIED` on import, read back rather than assumed. Size fits the **width** (216×242 from 1924×2154; squaring it would distort). It landed **top-right on `vitasilk` because the corner rule rejected top-left as occupied** — the rule working, not a fallback. The duration rule was first "one second after the last beep" (1.400 s, derived) and the user then ruled a flat **1.000 s**; `assertBeepsFitWatermark` retains the beep measurement as a guard so a future file whose beeps run long fails loudly instead of being cut mid-beep. Current margin 0.600 s.

17. **Three beeps, measured rather than taken on trust** (session 2). Count stable at 3 across 5–30% of peak, collapsing to 1 at 1–2% because the tails never fall below ~0.9% between them. The 30 ms merge gap is measured, not chosen: each beep is a two-pulse tone with ~22 ms internal pulses against ~33 ms between beeps. Last beep ends 0.400 s.

18. **The image cache no longer keys on `modeVersion`** (session 1). Enumerating what the image call actually reads showed every candidate field already in the key as prompt text: `stylePrompt`, palette and the variation axes reach the request only through `slot.prompt`, negatives only through `slot.negativePrompt`, both hashed verbatim. A content hash could only differ when those strings already differ, and would wrongly invalidate a slot when an axis value it never drew was removed — the four Block 4 bake-off entries would have been unmigratable. **The mode was removed from the key rather than content-hashed.** All 14 stranded entries migrated free; `aspectRatio` is not stored in the manifests, so it was recovered by recomputing the old key and requiring it to reproduce the directory name — 14/14 did at 1:1. **$2.064064 was on disk**, more than the ~$1.55 the Block 6 handoff estimated, which counted only the production run.

19. **A build refuses to run on a dead pointer** (session 5). Session 1's cache re-key left every plan's candidate path dead and the first full build **silently skipped 4 of 5 images**. Nothing was lost — the files were all there under new keys — but nothing warned; it was found by trying. Every referenced path is now verified before any AE work, all missing paths reported together, and the build fails rather than producing a comp with gaps. It fails in TypeScript before any `DoScript`, so AE is never touched — a deliberate deviation from the ES3 error contract the goal specified, because failing earlier is better.

20. **`sourceText` was wrong on all 343 words of all five plans** (sessions 6–7). `plan-builder.ts` documents it as the anchored draft token but indexed positionally into a different array. Cosmetic today — nothing reads it — but Block 8's transcript editor is where it surfaces. ## Known issues & risks

- **The aligner mis-pairs across scripts, and it is the largest open defect in the project.** Written up in full at `docs/DEFECT-alignment-script-mismatch.md`. The Scribe draft is Arabic script and the corrected text is Arabizi, so every pair in a code-switched run is a **tied-cost substitution** and Levenshtein has no signal at all; the path among the ties is arbitrary. On `vitasilk` it deletes draft token `نم` and shifts every following substitution by one, putting `il` 0.540 s before its own token. **209 of 343 words — 61% — across 49 runs rest on a cross-script pairing the aligner had no evidence for.** A same-script-anchor fix was written and measured: it removed nearly every anchor, surviving Latin tokens paired across distance (`fih`→`vitamin`, `26`→`et`), producing a **three-token shift against the original one-token one**, seven zero-duration words and two duplicate intervals. It was discarded; `align.ts` is unchanged and no plan was written from it. A real fix needs transliteration-aware cost — ORTHOGRAPHY_GUIDE §2's character table is the named source. **And a second, separate defect: `align` has no many-to-one operation at all**, so the `26` → `نيرشعو`+`ةتس` merge takes one token's interval rather than both. A transliteration cost alone will not fix that.
- **The correspondence check passes, and that is the finding.** The aligner never gives a word an interval belonging to a token other than the one it *records*; it records the wrong pairing. A checker reading the aligner's own output cannot see this. Session 6's weaker check — "does this interval exist in the Scribe response" — passed 21/21 while the alignment was wrong, because an interval can be real and belong to a different word. **The check was too weak to have established what it was taken to establish**, and that reasoning was the conversation's, not Claude Code's.
