## Decisions made (and why)

1. **MediaPipe Image Segmenter is frozen; YOLO11-seg is assessed and rejected.** ARCHITECTURE §1.4 named YOLO as the fallback and Block 5 was to decide it. The user reviewed all five contact sheets (231 frames) and ruled the masks accurate. Rejecting it avoids installing ultralytics and torch and pinning a second ~1 GB model by sha256 across two machines for Block 10's golden run. **ultralytics must not be installed.**
2. **Zones derive from the mask, never from the bounding box.** Measured session 1: person pixels are ~0.25 of the frame while the median person bbox is ~0.64 of it — the mask fills about two-fifths of its own box. Box-derived zones would discard the real negative space beside the head and between the arms. `person_stats`' bbox stays as reported metadata and is not an input to zone derivation.
3. **`PERSON_COMPONENT_FLOOR` = 0.0001 of frame (52 px at 540×960).** The non-largest component distribution decays smoothly from 1 px with no natural gap, so the floor is a judgement taken low, on the asymmetric bias: a retained speck shrinks a zone, a dropped hand puts a generated image on a hand. 444 non-largest components across 231 frames, max 0.005833 — none reaches 1%. Every component the floor drops was verified by eye on rendered frames to be a hem or shoe fragment; no limb is dropped anywhere in the corpus.
4. **`BOTTOM_EXCLUSION` is a product rule, not a mask-defect fix.** It was introduced on the user's observation that a dress was under-covered at the frame bottom, on the hypothesis that low-contrast fabric under-covers generally. **The measurement refuted the premise**: bottom-15% coverage is *higher* than the rest of the frame on all five reels. The constant stays with its reason corrected — no image is ever placed at the bottom of a 9:16 frame — and it costs 0.0% of valid seconds.
5. **Asymmetric hysteresis, open 2 / close 1.** A closed zone costs a placement opportunity; a zone left open through a real intrusion puts an image on a hand. One sample is 0.5 s, long enough for a raised hand to be genuinely present. Chosen, not measured. Session 2 correctly reported that the brief contradicted itself here (goal 3 ruled close-1, goal 6 tested close-2), implemented the ruling, and wrote the test to assert what the code does; the close-2 test is retained as documentation of the alternative.
6. **The short edge, not area, is the zone predicate.** `MIN_ZONE_AREA` admitted vitasilk's left zone at 0.052 × 0.800 — 112 px wide on a 2160-wide frame, a strip no square image fits. Generated images are 1:1 (frozen Block 4) and image comps work at 1200×1200, so the binding dimension is `min(w, h)`. `MIN_ZONE_SHORT_EDGE` = 0.15 of frame width (324 px), replacing area outright.
   **Normalized units are anisotropic on 9:16** — `min(w, h)` on normalized values is wrong by 1.78× on one axis. The constant is in units of frame width, converting h through the aspect ratio. Caught by session 3, not by the brief.
7. **0.15 is held provisionally, on a ground the measurement uncovered.** The predicate applies per frame *before* hysteresis, so it re-cuts windows rather than only deleting zones: test-3's left went 253 px/one window → 449 px/two windows, ground-truth's right 285 px → 393+437 px. Fragmented validity is cheap because slots are timed; rect width is not. **The open question — whether a 324 px image reads as a stamp — is a hypothesis no available measurement settles**, and is revisited by the user's eye on a built comp in Block 7. Lowering it is a one-line change plus a free re-run.
8. **`MIN_PLACED_SHORT_EDGE` is authoritative; `MIN_ZONE_SHORT_EDGE` stays as a cheap pre-filter and is not raised to match.** They disagree — the zone predicate admits test-1's 345 px and 365 px side zones which, after card clearance and the 0.88 fill, cannot hold a placed square of 0.15 frame width. The floor applies to the placed rect because that is what the constant's stated reason describes: the image a viewer sees. Raising the zone predicate to match would bake `FILL_FRACTION` — an unmeasured constant — into zone geometry. The disagreement is documented rather than tuned away.
9. **Card and cutout footprints are not collapsed.** A card has a visible border, so it is inset from the zone edge — a border touching the boundary reads as a second frame cropped by the subject. A cutout's edge is the subject's own silhouette. `CARD_EDGE_CLEARANCE` = 0.02 of frame width (43 px), `CUTOUT_EDGE_CLEARANCE` = 0. Both chosen, not measured. Measured effect: vitasilk's one cutout placed at 508 px against cards at 390–430 px in the same zone. **A slot whose presentation is null takes the card footprint** — the more demanding one — because the gate sets presentation only when every candidate agrees.
10. **Jitter cannot escape its region by construction.** The square's side is drawn first, then its position from the travel that side leaves inside the safe region; there is nothing to clamp. Re-validated anyway. 200 seeds on a minimum-sized zone all stay inside the zone, the frame, the subtitle band and the bottom exclusion.
11. **Final-frame sampling** (user ruling). test-1's `img004` spans 19.719–21.940 s while every window ended at 21.5215 s — the last 2 fps sample — inside a 0.4671 s unobserved tail. Session 4 refused to extend the window into it and stopped, which was correct: extending would have invented validity for an interval nobody observed. The ruling was to **sample the final frame**, the only option that adds evidence. Each reel's last decodable frame is now appended, found by seeking and decoding rather than trusting a container frame count, flagged `final: true` and named `frame-final.png` so it can never be swept into the numbered grid. test-1's last observation is now 21.9553 s; the 0.0334 s remainder is one frame's own duration at 29.97 fps, so nothing is unobserved.
12. **The three-rectangle decomposition was replaced by maximal free rectangles.** The user ruled that vitasilk's top space was under-recognized. **Both hypotheses were measured before anything changed, and both hold — (a) decisively:**
    - *One frame governs a whole window.* The emitted rect is the intersection across the validity window. vitasilk's per-frame top rect runs min 547 / median 879 / max 1015 px, and **frame 14 at 7.007 s set 547 px for all 25.53 s** while 34 of 53 frames exceeded 800. Other reels lose only 12–16 px to intersection.
    - *The three kinds are structurally blind to the region beside the head* — below the top cut, inside the width of the arms. Free-area coverage 76–82% on four reels, **58% on vitasilk**, whose right rectangle captured nothing.

    Replacement: largest-rectangle-under-histogram over the occupancy grid, **ranked by `min(w,h)` rather than area** — the grid is isotropic and area rewards a strip no square fits. `kind` became a label derived from position; a rect fitting no label is dropped rather than given an invented enum value (`between` was named as what it would be called, and is not implemented).

    Result: vitasilk's largest square 547 → 816 px; test-1's side zones 345/365 → 624–656 px; zone count rose on every reel.
13. **Images may be placed over the body, never over the head or face** (user ruling). A deliberate departure from PROJECT_SPEC §4 ("negative space above the head and left/right") and ARCHITECTURE §5.5 (zones avoid the person). The user's reason: mid-to-lower torso is dead visual weight in a talking-head reel, and an image there reads as deliberate composition rather than obstruction.
    The enabler cost nothing: `selfie_multiclass_256x256` is a softmax over six categories, so **hair + face-skin gives a head mask with no new model and no new inference**. Session 1 had saved only the summed person confidence; the head mask is now written alongside it.
14. **Hair is head; no percentile relaxation** (user ruling). A torso zone must be clear of head pixels on **every** observed frame in its window, never 90% of them. The user was told this might mean vitasilk gets no torso zone and ruled that outcome correct. **It did**: vitasilk's head mask reaches y 0.9510 (long hair over the shoulders) and 48 of 53 frames have a head that alone blocks the whole band. Nothing was relaxed.
    Enforced as a property: a frame whose head drops lower either shrinks the window's rect or splits the window. A first docstring claimed the intersection always achieves this; **a test proved it doesn't** and the docstring was corrected.
15. **`HEAD_THRESHOLD` = 0.25, deliberately lower than the body mask's 0.5.** The head mask is a confidence map, and thresholding at 0.5 trims exactly the low-confidence pixels at hair edges and jaw boundaries — where under-coverage would come from. Over-including costs torso area; under-including puts an image on a chin.
    **Measured cost: it does not cost rect size** — the largest square is 3–7% *larger* than at 0.5 — **it costs contiguity**: one 21–23 s window becomes 4–10 windows totalling 10–14 s. A first measurement summed overlapping fragments and made 0.25 look 300–870% larger; that was meaningless and was corrected in the same report.
16. **Fragmentation is measured, not tuned.** Maximal rectangles raised zone counts sharply (vitasilk 2→20, test-1 3→18) with many sub-second windows. `MATCH_MIN_IOU` was not touched to hide it. **Measured cost for background zones: none** — every slot on both fixtures had more than one candidate zone. **Measured cost for torso zones: total** — test-1 has 6 torso zones and not one window contains a slot's span.
17. **A report cannot name the commit that contains it.** Session 1's Repo state was wrong not through carelessness but by shape: it wrote Repo state before committing itself. Recorded as a guidelines amendment; every subsequent report names HEAD at time of writing and states that its own commit follows.
